Players have to recruit sailors of various types to man their ships. Sailors have various class progression trees that determine what roles they fill and skills they provide. A sailor is represented as a single unit, filling a single sailor slot on a ship, but is comprised of hundreds of actual sailors. A brand new sailor unit consists of 100 Rookies. Doing their job well over time will convert Rookies into Seasoned, and further advancement will convert Seasoneds into Veterans. Seasoned and Veteran sailors provide bonuses to the sailors overall performance.

Sailors all have the same set of stats, but their class determines the growth rate for those stats, which can fluctuate over time as they take on more specialized roles, possibly becoming negative for certain stats (though the stat total itself can never fall below zero).

### Sailor Stats

- **Operations (OPR)**
  - Bridge Officer primary stat. Provides small bonuses to all other sailors and their performance.
- **Spotting (SPT)**
  - Determines max vision length and ability to spot incoming aircraft or torpedoes.
- **Accuracy (ACC)**
  - Primary gunnery attribute, determines shell deviation and overall accuracy when firing.
- **Reloading (RLD)**
  - Primary gunnery attribute, determines reload time of turret mounts or launchers.
- **Torpedoes (TRP)**
  - Primary attribute for torpedo launchers, determines accuracy and likelihood of malfunctions with torpedoes.
- **Anti-Air (AAW)**
  - Skill with small-arms fire and effectiveness of automatic anti-air turrets.
- **DMG (Damage Control)**
  - Effectiveness of damage control teams at putting out fires and prevent catastrophic damage from impacts.
- **REP (Repairs)**
  - Effectiveness of repair teams at undoing damage already done, and restoring lost hit points
- **ENG (Engineering)**
  - Effectiveness of managing the ship's engines and power systems. Determines total max speed and how much faster and for how long a ship can run in EEP (Emergency Engine Power) mode.
- **AIR (Aircraft)**
  - General aircraft stat. Used by all aircraft types for skill in handling their aircraft, especially spotter aircraft that use no other skill.
- **FTR (Fighters)**
  - Used by fighter aircraft, determines their capability at shooting down other aircraft and defending from other fighters or anti-air fire.
- **BMB (Bombers)**
  - Used by torpedo and dive bombers, determines their accuracy at dropping their payloads and for defending from fighters or anti-air fire.
- **DCK (Deck Operations)**
  - Used by deck crew, determines how quickly aircraft can be refueled, repaired and rearmed and ready for another sortie.

### Sailor Skills

Sailors also gain and lose certain skills as they progress, which are the primary means of determining what equipment sailors can use and what capabilities they have. Bridge Crew, for instance, are the only sailor type that have the BRIDGE skill, which allows them to man the bridge and access the ship progression tree. SM. GUNS is the first gunnery skill accessible by sailors, and allows them to use 4"-6" guns. MED. GUNS follows up, allowing access to 8"-10" guns. LG. GUNS after that, allows access to 11"-14" guns, and finally HG. GUNS allows sailors to utilize the largest guns, anything above 14". There are various other skills as well, which will be specified later.

### Sailor Generation

When a player wants to create a new sailor, they do so from the shipyard. They are offered a randomized set of stats for a generic sailor, including growth rates. They can reroll the stats as many times as they wish, for a small fee each time. The maximum growth rate for any stat is +12, and +15 for OPR. These growth rates will be modified many times as the sailors take on different roles. At level 1, an unclassed sailor is capable of manning neutral ship types as any role. At level 5, they will be able to class the sailor to a specific nation and select their initial role: Bridge Crew, Gunnery Officer, Support Officer, or Deck Crew. Bridge Crew never re-class again, but are then given access to the actual ship progression tree they will go down. Gunnery Officers have different progression paths based on their nation, but are the sailors that will be used in Primary, Secondary, or Torpedo slots. Support Officers will later class into various sub-classes, such as Damage Control, Repair Crews, or Engineers.

### Growth

Sailors gave EXP from doing well in combat, which is awarded at the end of matches. At various level thresholds, which are determined by the nationality of the sailor and their given progress tree, they will have the opportunity to switch to a new class. Doing so will grant them bonuses (as well as maluses) to their various growth rates, accelerating them in their specialization and curbing their growth in non-related fields, eventually causing them to lose skill in those areas, once the growth rate crosses below zero. Each level, a sailor's growth rates are applied to their stats. A stat cannot go below zero, even if a growth rate value for that stat can - no sailor will negatively effect a ship's overall performance because of their specialty.

### Crew Composition

At level 1, sailors are created with a composition of 100 Rookies. Over time, these rookies can be converted into seasoned sailors through well-aimed shots, successful damage control actions, and other well-executed actions that fall within that sailor's job description. Furthermore, as units become more developed over time, with the use of special end-game training items, these seasoned sailors can become veterans. Seasoned sailors confer a bonus to the overall skill values of the sailor unit, in accordance to how much of the unit is comprised of seasoned sailors over rookies. Accordingly, veterans do the same, but provide an even larger bonus. If a sailor unit is missing sailors from its composition, this will induce a performance penalty in much the same way. New rookies can be hired for a small fee to fill out missing crew composition in a sailor unit. Rookies themselves do not provide any positive or negative modifiers to a sailor unit's performance. Explosions, flooding compartments, and other catastrophic damage has the chance to kill sailors of various skill levels within a unit, though seasoned sailors and veterans are more skilled at avoiding death than rookies are, but they are not immune to it.
